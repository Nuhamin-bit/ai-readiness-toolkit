How to use this toolkit

This toolkit helps a small business or nonprofit with 5 to 50 staff go from “we have AI tools” to “our team uses them safely and well” in four weeks. No AI or IT specialist is needed. One person, the facilitator, runs the program.

The four abilities

AbilityWhat it meansSign it’s missing
DescriptionExplaining a task to AI with enough context to get a useful answer“AI never gives me what I want”
DelegationDeciding which tasks, or which steps, AI should do and which stay with a personAI used for everything, or for nothing
DiscernmentChecking AI output for errors, invented facts and biasAI answers copied without review
DiligenceUsing AI safely, legally and transparentlyCustomer or private data pasted into free tools

Why safe use comes first: staff start using AI on day one, so the rules must come before the skills.

What you need

  • One 60-minute session per week, in person or on a video call
  • An AI tool the organization already has or approves (for example Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude)
  • A shared document as a “prompt library,” where staff save requests that worked

Session guides · Weeks 1 and 2

Session 1: Diligence

Goal Staff can use AI without exposing private data.

0–10 minWhy safe use comes first. Share one real example of an AI data leak or mistake in the news.
10–30 minWalk through the Safe-Use Policy together. Fill in the approved tools and contact person.
30–50 minPractice: sort 10 sample requests into “OK for AI” and “Not OK.” Discuss the tricky ones.
50–60 minEveryone signs or acknowledges the policy. Questions.

Facilitator tip: make reporting mistakes safe. People who fear blame hide problems.

Session 2: Description

Goal Staff get a useful first draft in one or two tries.

0–15 minThe four parts of a good request: task, context, audience, format. Show a weak and a strong request side by side.
15–25 minHow to fix a weak answer: say what was wrong (“too long,” “too formal”) instead of starting over.
25–50 minPractice: each person rewrites one of their chosen use cases as a four-part request and compares results.
50–60 minAdd the best requests to the shared prompt library.
Weak: “Summarize this.”
Strong: “Summarize this report in 5 bullet points for our board, who aren’t technical. Flag any decisions they need to make.”

Session guides · Weeks 3 and 4

Session 3: Delegation

Goal Staff can decide what to hand to AI and what to keep.

0–15 minWhat AI does well (drafting, summarizing, reformatting) and what stays with people (decisions about people, money, legal and safety matters).
15–45 minPractice: map one chosen use case into steps. Mark each step “AI drafts,” “Person does,” or “Person checks.”
45–60 minShare maps. Agree on one new workflow to try this week.

Session 4: Discernment

Goal Staff catch AI mistakes before they spread.

0–15 minHow AI gets things wrong: invented facts and sources, outdated information, confident tone, bias.
15–40 minPractice: find the errors in three prepared AI answers, each with one planted mistake.
40–50 minChecking habits: open every source, double-check numbers and names, ask a person when stakes are high.
50–60 minEveryone retakes the readiness check (Worksheet 1).
Preparing the practice answers: ask an AI tool a question you know the answer to, then edit in one wrong number, one fake source or one wrong name. Keep an answer key.

Template · Share in Week 1

AI Safe-Use Policy

Organization:   Effective date:

Approved tools

Only these AI tools may be used for work:

Never put into an AI tool

  • Customer or client names, addresses, phone numbers or emails
  • Payment, bank or credit card details
  • Health or medical information
  • Passwords, login details or security settings
  • Employee records, salaries or performance reviews
  • Contracts or documents marked confidential

Always

  • Check facts, numbers and names before using an AI answer.
  • Have a person review anything sent to customers, funders or the public.
  • Remove names and details before pasting text in (“a customer” instead of a name).
  • Be open about AI’s role in important work when asked.

AI never makes the final decision on

Hiring, pay, pricing, refunds, legal or safety matters.

Questions or mistakes

Contact:

Reporting a mistake early is always the right call and will not be punished.

Staff acknowledgment:   Date:

These rules follow common practice from the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, scaled down for a small team. They are not legal advice. Organizations that handle health or payment data should also check the rules that apply to their sector.

About this toolkit

The AI Readiness Toolkit was created by Nuhamin H. Tesfay, an AI governance and cybersecurity researcher. It turns research on AI fluency, the Cybersecurity & AI Capital Model (CCM), published in the Proceedings of the Southeastern INFORMS Conference 2026, into a practical program for small teams. The four abilities follow the AI Fluency framework developed by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller.

You are free to use and share this toolkit with credit. It is a learning resource, not legal or compliance advice.
